Which of these six thousand proteins is worth a bench scientist's time?

I designed the whole funnel. Six thousand-plus sequences through three independent prediction tools at a 70% confidence floor, narrowing to roughly 700 — then convergence: time-course RNA-seq differential expression, secretion prediction across 863 proteins, and Swiss-Prot homology cross-validation to resolve which candidates were genuinely secreted. Six nominated — three with converging multi-evidence support, three novel — all now in wet-lab validation. Safety ran alongside: 54 virulence hits and 53 mobile genetic elements screened across a 5,800-gene genome, no major human-pathogenicity determinants, cleared for downstream development.

What does a protein do when sequence homology has nothing to say about it?

Domains of Unknown Function are exactly that — annotated as unknown because nothing similar has been characterized. Structure still has something to say. I built a pipeline pairing Pfam/InterPro assignment with AlphaFold2 prediction, then searching the resulting folds with Foldseek against local MMseqs2 databases hosted on AWS, recovering function where BLAST comes back blank.

What separates an NK cell that kills from one that doesn't?

Six healthy donors, twelve cytotoxic and non-cytotoxic samples, roughly a million cells. QC, normalization, clustering, cell-type annotation, marker discovery, differential expression, pathway enrichment — then Monocle trajectories to pull the pseudotime-associated expression programs and cell-state markers that actually distinguish the two states. I layered a MeRIP-seq framework on top to ask whether RNA methylation and transcript-isoform shifts track with immune function.

Can a model tell us why an exon gets skipped, not just whether?

Accuracy on its own tells a biologist nothing. I built an ETL pipeline over ENCODE shRNA knockdown data for RNA-binding proteins, opening up differential splicing across more than 10,000 splice junctions, then trained XGBoost over a graph-based representation of mRNA splicing spanning 200+ RBPs. SHAP attribution turned the model back into readable regulatory logic — which proteins matter, where, in what combination. The analysis contributed to a 2026 bioRxiv preprint.
